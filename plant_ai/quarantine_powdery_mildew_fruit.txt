# Quarantined training images — powdery_mildew_fruit label decontamination
#
# Removed from dataset/train/powdery_mildew_fruit/ on 2 October 2026 after a visual audit of all
# 81 real training images plus an objective lesion-texture measurement. These 13 images are grey
# mould (Botrytis cinerea) appearances filed under powdery mildew (Podosphaera aphanis).
#
# WHY: powdery mildew presents as fine, even, MATTE WHITE powder - smooth inside the lesion with
# high luminance. Botrytis presents as textured grey-brown mycelium. Measured against the
# gray_mold training distribution, these 13 fell inside its interquartile range for lesion
# roughness (4.0-9.3) AND luminance (0.565-0.692) with a brown-grey lesion share > 0.5, i.e. they
# are statistically and visually indistinguishable from grey mould. Visual review agreed
# independently: powdery_mildew_fruit_120.jpg is a dried brown shrivelled mummy (that one was
# identified by eye, and was outside the automated 13), and _15.jpg / _44.jpg show grey-brown fuzz.
#
# The model had been trained to associate grey-brown fruit with powdery mildew, which is why it
# called gray_mold at 0.92 on genuine mildew berries. Removing them raised that class from 50% to
# 58.3% accuracy and cut confident wrong answers from 11 to 9.
#
# DISPOSITION: quarantined rather than deleted, so the decision is reversible. These were NOT
# relabelled into gray_mold, because borderline cases that cannot be confidently identified
# should not be asserted into the class being protected.
#
# The originals remain in D:\ivp\union_dataset\powdery_mildew_fruit\ - this dataset is derived,
# so the source images were never at risk.
#
